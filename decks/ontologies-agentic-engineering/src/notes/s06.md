Some of you are skeptical of symbolic AI, and you have good reason to be. This timeline takes that skepticism seriously.

It starts in 1956 at Dartmouth, where symbolic logic began as an approach to AI. The early bet was to write knowledge down as explicit symbols and rules, and let a program reason over them.

[next]

In the 1980s came expert systems, and here the line breaks downward. Expert systems failed because hand-coding a whole open world does not scale. Every new case needed a person to write another rule, and the world kept producing new cases faster than anyone could write them.

[next]

From 2012 to 2024 the dotted line rises. That is neural scale. Neural nets scale with GPUs and handle ambiguity, which the symbolic systems never did. They are also probabilistic, and hallucination is the other side of probabilistic generation. The sampling that lets a model handle a vague request also lets it produce a confident wrong answer.

[next]

The last hold is 2026, labelled neurosymbolic. The dotted neural line and a solid symbolic line run in parallel through the orange dot.

The difference from the 1980s is scope. The current approach does not model the universe. It puts a small symbolic boundary around the specific invariants that break things. In a billing system that might be a refund limit, a role that must never be confused with another role, or a decision record that a newer one replaced. The neural model handles everything outside that boundary. Keep the symbolic part small and point it at the rules that have already caused incidents.

Now let's shift from theory to practice: how do you build an engineering environment around coding agents so they ship at volume without producing slop?
