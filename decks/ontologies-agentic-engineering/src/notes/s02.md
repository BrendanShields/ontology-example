The word ontology sounds like a philosophy seminar. You already use several of them every day without noticing. I'll show four, and each one is about ordinary people.

Start with one person. This is Maya. On her own she is a profile, a row in a table.

[next]

This is Tom. Maya knows Tom. That one line between them is the whole idea of a social graph, and they both look pleased about it.

[next]

Now Priya turns up. Priya knows Tom. Nobody has told the app anything about Maya and Priya.

[next]

Watch the dot. It has followed the first link from Maya to Tom. The only way on to Priya is through Tom, so in it goes. Tom was not expecting that, and neither were the other two.

[next]

Out the other side, along the second link, and it arrives at Priya. Tom is confused for a second and then gets on with his day. That is what being a node in someone else's graph query feels like.

[next]

Two steps, so the app suggests Priya under people you may know. It didn't guess from their names or their photos. FOAF, which stands for friend of a friend, is the small vocabulary behind this. It says each of these is a Person and each line is knows. Because the links have types, a friend of a friend is a walk across the graph.

[next]

Friends are not the only route. All three of them work at Acme, and so does Ben. Works at is another typed link, this time from a Person to an Organization.

[next]

Maya has never met Ben. But the graph says they work at the same company and they hold the same role, Designer. That is enough for the app to suggest him as well. Be clear about who did what. The app wrote the rule, same company and same role. The vocabulary did not. What the vocabulary gave the app is facts with types, so the rule could be one line and give the same answer every time.

[next]

Second one. Maya is hungry, and she is thinking about what to eat.

[next]

She wants something quick, so she searches for exactly that. Two words, quick dinner.

[next]

Here is the search engine without any help. It finds three recipe pages, and each one has a number next to the word time. Three, twenty, ninety. The smallest is three, so it offers Maya a beef stew. That stew takes three hours.

[next]

Now the same pages with Schema.org. Each one states a prepTime, and the value is a typed duration. Three hours, twenty minutes, ninety minutes. Look at the dashed line under the search box as well. The ontology has no idea what quick means. The engine decided that quick means under half an hour. That is its guess, and another engine might say fifteen minutes. What the ontology adds is that the guess can be checked against every recipe in the same way.

[next]

The rating is read the same way, as a ratingValue and not as a loose number on a page. And there is Maya's dinner, four and a half stars and twenty minutes.

[next]

Third. Tom says play Mercury.

[next]

There are at least three things called Mercury. A planet, a chemical element and a musician. A person works out which one from the word play. Software that only has the word cannot.

[next]

Wikidata gives each of them its own identifier and says what each one is. Q308 is an instance of a planet. Q925 is a chemical element. Q15869 is a human whose occupation is singer. The dashed part is again the assistant's own reading. It takes play to mean it needs a musician. The typed facts then leave one candidate standing.

[next]

So Tom gets Freddie Mercury, and the assistant never looks up the weather on a planet.

[next]

Last one, and it is the serious one. Sam has had a heart attack.

[next]

His GP writes heart attack. The emergency department writes MI. The cardiologist writes myocardial infarction. A system that matches on text sees three different conditions for one man.

[next]

SNOMED CT lists all three wordings as synonyms of one concept, 22298006. Now Sam has one condition and he is counted once, in his own record and in every statistic built on it.

[next]

The concept also has parents. In SNOMED a myocardial infarction is a kind of ischemic heart disease, and a couple of steps further up that is a kind of heart disease. So when the heart clinic asks who has heart disease, Sam is on the list. Nobody ever wrote those two words in his notes. FIBO does the same kind of work for financial terms.

These are working products, not academic exercises. In each one a system made a guess about what a person meant, and an ontology gave it typed facts to check the guess against. Keep that in mind, because a coding agent in your repository has the same problem.
