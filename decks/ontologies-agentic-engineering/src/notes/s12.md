I'll walk one turn of the write path. Frank Coyle teaches generative AI at UC Berkeley. In his AI Engineer World's Fair 2026 talk he summarized this pattern as "Pydantic at the door, ontology at the ledger, and pure agents" — the agent proposes, the ontology permits.

LLMs never execute tools directly; they only propose parameters, and the orange dot is that proposal. Gate 1 is Pydantic at the door, checking parameter types and ranges. Then comes the framed box, dry_run(), keeping the agent pure by computing what the action would do with no side effects.

[next]

Gate 2 checks that candidate result at the ledger against OWL rules and SHACL shapes. The blocking SHACL validation is our addition on top of Coyle's OWL reasoner. Here it catches a double refund and bounces structured feedback back to the agent — which rule failed and on which record — before anything touches production.

[next]

The agent repairs the proposal and tries again. This time the result passes both gates, and the change is staged for review.

[next]

The dashed rail on top is a decision model watching the loop. As Coyle notes via Böhm and Jacopini, sequence, conditionals and loops make an agent loop Turing-complete — which is why agents can spin or drift off-task. The risk check asks in milliseconds whether a call looks risky enough for a human, and the drift check watches whether the loop is spinning unproductively.

I'll repeat the boundary once. The decision model gives a calibrated guess, so it never replaces Gate 2. The decision model decides how carefully to look, and Gate 2 decides what is allowed.

The gates decide what the agent may write. Next is the read path: what it gets to see.
