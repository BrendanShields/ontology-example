Before we wire up any routers or gates, start with the engineering goal. Lauren Tan, who goes by poteto and wrote pstack, put it bluntly at AI Engineer CODE 2026: automating a sloppy inner loop just produces more slop. Her team went from reviewing about 3 pull requests a day in 2024 to closer to 300, reaching 11,000 a month while holding the revert rate at about 0.5%. One write-up of her talk also reports comments in the codebase dropped from 65,824 to 1,227, about 98% fewer — treat that figure as secondhand. Her argument, which she calls software gardening, is that the repository is the biggest input to the next agent, so workarounds and stale comments compound.

[next]

Here is how she keeps trust at that volume. She calls this an inverted trust pyramid, with the heaviest investment at the widest band. Codebase and API design come first, banning nuance-heavy patterns deterministically. Static analysis comes next: lints, types, compilers and tests. Verification artifacts come third, where she cites colleague Cindy Sridharan's figure that roughly 70% of daily tokens go to verification. Prose — AGENTS.md and skills — comes fourth, and human review is the narrowest layer at the bottom. A person reviews evidence instead of a 400-line diff.

[next]

The step from her approach to a Git-versioned ontology is my synthesis, not hers. If you strip out 98% of comments and keep CLAUDE.md tiny, the business logic and the architectural why need a cleaner home.

Business logic goes in LinkML YAML, which compiles to Python types, validation shapes and Markdown docs from one source. The architectural why goes in a small graph of decision records, about 10 to 20 classes. Both live in Git and change through pull requests.

To run that harness at high volume without blowing up latency and cost, the next question is which layer of the system does which job.
