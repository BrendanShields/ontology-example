This slide draws on Lauren Tan, who goes by poteto and wrote pstack. At AI Engineer in 2026 she gave a talk called All Code Is Slop, Except Mine. The numbers come from that talk. She went from reviewing about 3 pull requests a day in 2024 to closer to 300. Her team as a whole handles about 11,000 pull requests a month, and the revert rate settled at about 0.5%. One write-up of the talk reports that comments in the codebase went from 65,824 to 1,227, about 98% fewer. That figure comes from a write-up of her slide, so treat it as secondhand. Her argument uses a gardening analogy. The repository is the biggest input to the next agent, so sloppy patterns and stale comments compound.

Here is where that trust comes from. She calls this an inverse trust pyramid. The widest band, where trust goes first, is the codebase and API design. Static analysis comes next, meaning lints, types, compilers and tests. Verification artifacts come after that. Prose comes fourth, meaning rules, skills and AGENTS.md. Human review comes last. She says most of her tokens go to verification, and she cites Cindy Sridharan's figure of about 70%. The human then reviews evidence instead of a 400-line diff.
