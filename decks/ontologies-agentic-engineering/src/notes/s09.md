Here are the names I have avoided so far, the four standards Netflix used. I'll take them one at a time.

RDF stores facts as triples. Each fact has a subject, a predicate and an object. ADR_005 supersedes ADR_004 is one triple.

[next]

SPARQL is the query language over those triples. It runs exact multi-hop queries. Ask what replaced ADR_001 and the query walks the supersedes links back to ADR_005.

[next]

The next two are where I see the most common mistake, which is treating them as the same thing. OWL works in an open world. If a fact is unstated, OWL treats it as unknown, not false. OWL is for inference, using property chains like grandfatherOf and contradictions like disjointWith. Here it infers that ADR_005 replaces ADR_001, which nobody wrote down. It never complains that something is missing.

[next]

SHACL works in a closed world. If a required field is missing, that is a violation. SHACL validates the shape of the data. For example, every Decision needs a rationale of at least 20 characters and an ISO date, and a Decision without them is refused. The two are complementary. OWL tells you what follows from the data, and SHACL tells you whether the data is complete enough to use.

[next]

These four are what Netflix built its map on, the one from the home screen story. This is the detail I would copy first. Their post says they refused a generic Service type. Across twelve namespaces and a software catalog of roughly 49,000 entries, every app has one concrete type. Once a generic type exists, new things get filed under it because it is the easy choice, and then the type tells you nothing.

So far this is all nouns and facts. An agent also needs verbs.
