Start with what most of us do today. One large model answers every question the system has. Which package does this task belong to? Is this action risky? Is the refund within the limit? Each of those is another model call, with its latency and its cost. I turned off my own coding agent's auto mode because its LLM risk checks were too sluggish.

[next]

The quick questions can go to a decision model. It is non-generative. It takes the state plus a typed question and returns a calibrated probability in one pass. TypeSafe AI launched Jev on 15 September 2026, and OpenAI announced its Decisions API on 29 September. Jev's question types are Choice, Score and Noul. Noul is the yes or no type, and OpenAI calls it Predicate. TypeSafe reports up to about 200 times faster and 400 times cheaper than an LLM call, and that is the vendor's own top-of-range figure. Browserbase reported in early testing that the median latency of its act() step fell from 1.97 seconds to 0.46 seconds, about 4.3 times, with an LLM fallback below a confidence threshold. LangChain's router post on 1 October found routing cut median cost by 64% with no significant change in merged-PR rate.

The refund limit is a different kind of question. It has one right answer, and a guess is not acceptable. A decision model guarantees the shape of its output, not its truth, and it can't do arithmetic or graph logic. So hard limits go to symbolic rules, meaning ontologies, types and lints. They are deterministic and take milliseconds.

That leaves the large model with the work only it can do, which is open-ended synthesis such as writing the change. LangChain quotes Jaya Gupta's phrase for this, cheap by default, frontier on exception. Symbolic checks own the hard invariants, decision models own the fast fuzzy calls, and the frontier model handles the rest.
